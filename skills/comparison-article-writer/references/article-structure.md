# Comparison Article Structure & Quality Reference

Use this as the default skeleton. Adapt section order and depth to the brief, audience, and word count. Not every section is required for short articles.

## Match the SERP-winning pattern first

Before applying this structure, recall the SERP brief from Step 2 of SKILL.md. Adapt length and **perspective** to what ranks:
- **Experience-led winners** → write in first person from hands-on testing (e.g., "After running both for a month…"), using the user's real usage notes where provided, or a clearly framed tester perspective.
- **Objective winners** → stay neutral and data-driven, comparing features, prices, and specs without personal narrative.
- **Hybrid** → lead objectively but add experience-based callouts.

## Images (3-4 required)

Embed 3-4 images alongside the structure below (see Step 4 in SKILL.md for generation/routing rules):
- **Hero image** after the introduction.
- **Comparison infographic** immediately after the comparison table.
- **Data chart** (Python/matplotlib from the fact table) in the most data-heavy criterion section.
- **Supporting photo or screenshot** in another key criterion section.
Each image needs an italic caption and descriptive alt text.

## Recommended structure

### 1. Title
- Match search intent. Common high-performing patterns:
  - `X vs Y: Which Is Better in [Year]?`
  - `X vs Y: [N] Key Differences (And Which to Choose)`
  - `Best [Category]: X vs Y vs Z Compared`
  - For roundups: `[N] Best [Category] Alternatives in [Year]`
- Include the primary keyword and the current year when relevant.

### 2. Introduction (100–150 words)
- State the comparison and who it's for in the first 1–2 sentences.
- **The short answer / verdict up front:** give the bottom line immediately (e.g., "X is best for most teams; choose Y if you need Z").
- Briefly preview what the article covers. Avoid long throat-clearing.

### 3. Quick comparison table (mandatory)
- Compare **5-8 features**, and **always include a pricing/plan row**. Choose the most decision-relevant criteria from the brief (e.g., price, free tier, best for, collaboration, standout feature, ease of use, integrations).
- One row per feature (or per option, whichever is cleaner), with a clear column per option.
- Add a "Best for" or "Our pick" row. This table is the most-screenshotted, most-scanned part — make it accurate, current, and skimmable, and ensure it never contradicts the body text.

### 4. At-a-glance: what each option is (short)
- One short paragraph per option: what it is, who makes it, primary use case, and the type of user it suits. No deep detail yet.

### 5. Head-to-head by criterion (the core)
- Create one subsection per major criterion from the brief, in priority order. Example: Pricing, Features, Ease of use, Integrations, Support, Performance.
- In each subsection: compare the options directly, name a winner *for that criterion*, and explain why in plain language. Use concrete numbers and examples, not vague adjectives.
- This is where balance matters: each option should win some rounds and lose others.

### 6. Pros and cons
- A short pros/cons list per option. Keep it honest — fabricated weaknesses or token cons erode trust.

### 7. Pricing comparison (if relevant)
- Break down plans/tiers with real, current numbers. Note free tiers, trials, hidden costs, and what each tier unlocks. Always date-check pricing.

### 8. Use-case recommendations / The verdict
- The most important section. Translate the comparison into decisions:
  - "**Choose X if** you… / value… / need…"
  - "**Choose Y if** you… / are… / want…"
  - For 3+ options, give a clear pick for each main persona.
- Restate the overall winner (or explicitly say it's a tie and why), with a one-line justification.

### 9. FAQ (optional, good for SEO)
- 3–6 short Q&As covering real questions ("Is X cheaper than Y?", "Can I migrate from X to Y?", "Which is better for beginners?"). Helps capture long-tail search and featured snippets.

### 10. Conclusion + CTA
- One tight paragraph restating the recommendation. End with the requested call to action (try, sign up, read related post) if provided.

## Quality checklist (verify before delivering)

- [ ] The verdict appears in the first ~150 words AND at the end.
- [ ] Every option has genuine strengths and honest weaknesses.
- [ ] A scannable comparison table is present and matches the body text (no contradictions).
- [ ] Recommendations are tied to specific use cases / personas, not "it depends."
- [ ] All prices, specs, and feature claims are current and sourced; anything unverified is flagged.
- [ ] Disclosed any bias (affiliate, ownership, sponsorship) if applicable.
- [ ] Differentiated from competing articles (fresher data, real testing, decision aid, or unique angle).
- [ ] Tone, length, and format match the brief.
- [ ] 3-4 relevant images are embedded (hero, comparison infographic, data chart, supporting photo), each with an italic caption and descriptive alt text; charts use real data, not AI generation.
- [ ] If SEO requested: primary keyword in title, intro, one H2, and meta description; natural usage, no stuffing.
- [ ] Scannable: short paragraphs, descriptive H2/H3s, bullet lists, bolded key takeaways.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Feature-dumping** without telling the reader what matters or who should care.
- **Fake balance** (one obvious winner with a token "con") or **fake neutrality** (refusing to recommend anything).
- **Stale or invented data** — the fastest way to lose credibility.
- **Burying the answer** at the bottom of a 2,000-word article.
- **Identical pros/cons** that don't actually differentiate the options.
- **Ignoring the reader's stage** — a "ready to buy" reader needs pricing and a verdict, not a 101 explainer.
