# Alternative Blog Article Template (v2)

This template produces decision-driven alternative articles that are distinct from generic listicles. The structure leads with context (what the category is, why people switch), then treats each tool as a mini-comparison against the main product rather than a standalone feature dump.

## Template Rules

1. **The opening is exactly 2 paragraphs with no heading**, followed immediately by a comparison table.
2. **Comparison table is mandatory.** Placed directly after the 2 intro paragraphs. Maximum 4 columns. Standardize all pricing to monthly figures.
3. **"What Are X?" section is exactly 2 paragraphs.** Educates newer readers and adds topical depth for SEO.
4. **"Why Consider Alternatives?" is exactly 2 paragraphs.** Validates the reader's intent and builds trust.
5. **Per-tool structure is fixed:** Price → Introduction → 4 Strengths → 4 Trade-offs → 3 Distinct Comparison Points → Best For (bullet point). Do not deviate.
6. **Pricing is mandatory in each product section.** Display as bold `**Price: $X**` immediately after the H2 heading and before the introduction paragraph. Include discount notes or free tier info in parentheses if applicable.
7. **"Best for" is always a single bullet point** at the end of each tool section. It names a persona or scenario, not a generic phrase.
8. **FAQ contains exactly 5 questions.** Mirror "People Also Ask" phrasing. Answer each in 2-3 direct sentences.
9. **Trade-offs must be honest.** Never write filler trade-offs like "could be better." State real limitations.
10. **Comparison points must be specific and factual.** Use numbers, feature names, or pricing — not vague claims.
11. **Short paragraphs only.** No paragraph longer than 4 lines.
12. **No corporate jargon.** Write like a consultant advising a colleague.

## Hard bans
1. Company revenue, valuation, user counts, acquisition history. Strictly what the tool does.
2. Vendor's own product page cited as a review.
3. Em-dashes.
4. Any paragraph over 4 lines.
5. More than 4 strengths or 4 trade-offs per tool.
6. Jargon a general reader would pause on (e.g., "leverage", "utilize").
7. More than 4 columns in the comparison table.
